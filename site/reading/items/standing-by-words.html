Standing by Words — Wendell Berry

Created December 31, 2013 · Updated November 12, 2026 · 196 words

In his essay “Standing by Words,” Berry identifies two “epidemic illnesses”: the “disintegration of communities” and the “disintegration of persons.” Then he says they are paralleled by a third kind of disintegration, which is the breaking down of language. Most of the essay focuses on this third problem.

Berry gives three conditions for meaningful language:

  1. It must “designate its object precisely”
  2. Its speaker/writer must “believe it, be accountable for it, be willing to act on it”
  3. It must be “conventional” (there must be a community that knows what it is)

Berry’s answer to the problem of language breaking down is love.

He closes by contrasting two ways of speaking about the future: making a prediction and making a promise.

This is the question: “Are we willing to stand by our words?” This is the test Berry ran over 40 years ago on English textbooks and government transcripts, and it works just as well for us, right now, with chat windows open in the next tab.
