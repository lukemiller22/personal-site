The Syntopicon — Mortimer J. Adler

Created August 12, 2019 · Updated November 18, 2026 · 748 words

The Syntopicon is a collection of topics. It is an index of ideas. It was originally published as the two-volume index to _Great Books of the Western World_, an Encyclopaedia Britannica collection of works arranged chronologically, beginning with Homer and stretching all the way into the 20th century.

An American philosopher named Mortimer Adler provided the original inspiration for the Syntopicon, and he was given the task of cataloguing and indexing the ideas. It was a massive undertaking that took more time and money than anyone expected. By the time it was completed, the project had taken 10 years, $2 million, and 400,000 hours of work from a team of over 100 readers.

The Syntopicon contains almost 3,000 topics organized under 102 ideas. Together, they trace the fundamental problems and questions that human beings have wrestled with for centuries.

Adler and his team believed that the Syntopicon contained the fundamental ideas found in the Great Books: ideas like Beauty, Education, Good and Evil, Justice, Love, Religion, Science, and Wisdom. These ideas address the questions that every one of us asks in the course of our lives.

In the Syntopicon, each of these great ideas is given its own section. The centerpiece of each section is an essay written by Adler. With his characteristic evenhandedness, Adler uses these essays to introduce us to the key points of view on each of these great ideas. At the end of each essay, he provides an outline of topics, references, cross-references, and additional readings.

The Syntopicon is not a concordance. It doesn’t list every occurrence of these words in the Great Books. Instead, it serves as a guide for tracing how people have discussed these ideas, sometimes using different language and often coming to very different conclusions.
