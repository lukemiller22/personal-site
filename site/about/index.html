About me

I’m Luke. Christian, husband, and father of four. Co-founder of Buildonline.

My interests are, alas, much wider than my expertise. I believe the kids would call this having too many side quests. I spend a lot of time reading and thinking about theology, philosophy, literature, history, technology, and how these things fit together.

I enjoy good books, old ideas, national parks, iced coffee (year-round), tools for thought, and family time in all its glorious varieties. I’m much worse at chess and piano than I should be considering how long I’ve spent playing both.

I’m especially interested in the questions, stories, symbols, and practices that shape the way we see the world. I think it’s far more interesting (and helpful) to read the record of human thought as a long conversation about what we ought to believe, remember, imagine, and do.

About the site

For many years, I’ve scattered notes across a patchwork of blogging sites, social media platforms, forum threads, and note-taking apps. Some of those places are still around, but many of them have disappeared. This site is intended to be a more permanent home for things I’ve written, collected, and read.

It’s also my small contribution to the open web. Years ago, I read Alan Jacobs’ essay, Tending the Digital Commons, and I tried to create a personal website. I just didn’t have the time (or skill) to get it working the way I wanted. Now, I have the help of Codex and Claude, so I can do in minutes what would have taken me hours before. My current workflow is to write and edit in markdown files using Obsidian. Then, I (or I guess we) publish it with Eleventy.

Every post has dates for created and updated, because I have been greatly influenced by the digital gardening community. For all of the writing posts, you will also see a status bar that shows its stage of development and level of completeness. This means that you will sometimes find unfinished and incomplete posts. There is an element of working with the garage door open here. I am writing first to my future self. I want to make sure I remember what I found interesting or important. I hope that anyone reading will also find it interesting or important.
